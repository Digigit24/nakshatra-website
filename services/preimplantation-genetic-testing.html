Types Of PGT

Types of preimplantation genetic testing

The original PGT types are organized into readable cards for quick comparison across desktop and mobile.

PGT-A (Aneuploidy)

This test screens embryos for chromosomal abnormalities. Healthy embryos should contain 23 pairs of chromosomes, one from the egg and one from the sperm.

PGT-M (Monogenic Disorders)

Targets specific inherited diseases when one or both partners are carriers or there is a family history. It supports transferring unaffected embryos and breaking the chain of transmission.

PGT-SR (Structural Rearrangements)

Used when structural chromosome rearrangements are part of the evaluation, helping guide embryo transfer planning.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Preimplantation Genetic Testing (PGT) is an advanced embryo screening technique performed during an IVF cycle to identify genetic or chromosomal abnormalities before embryo transfer. It ensures that only healthy embryos are selected for implantation, improving IVF success rates and reducing the risk of miscarriage or inherited genetic disorders. This test is especially helpful for couples facing infertility or repeated IVF failures.

The main benefits of Preimplantation Genetic Testing include higher IVF success and implantation rates, reduced chances of miscarriage, prevention of inherited genetic diseases, fewer IVF cycles needed to achieve pregnancy, and selection of the healthiest embryos for transfer.

The success rate of PGT with IVF depends on factors like maternal age, embryo quality, and lab expertise. On average, couples undergoing PGT experience implantation rates of 65-80%, compared to 40-50% without testing. Clinics with experienced embryologists and genetic counselors, such as Nakshatra Clinic in Baner, Pune, consistently achieve higher pregnancy success rates.

Though highly accurate (up to 98%), PGT is not 100% foolproof. Some embryos may yield inconclusive results or have mosaic patterns. Additionally, a small chance of false positive or false negative exists. Consulting a genetic counselor before and after the test helps interpret results correctly and plan next steps confidently.

PGT is usually scheduled during an IVF or ICSI cycle, right after fertilization and before embryo transfer. Your fertility team will plan the exact timeline based on your cycle stage and hormonal profile.

While PGT significantly reduces the risk of passing on genetic or chromosomal disorders, it cannot guarantee a completely healthy baby. It screens for known abnormalities, but other factors, such as environment or lifestyle, can still influence pregnancy outcomes.
