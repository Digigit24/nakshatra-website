Overview

Understanding uterine adhesions and Asherman's Syndrome

Uterine adhesions are bands of scar tissue that can form inside the uterus and cause the uterine walls to stick together. They may develop after dilation and curettage (D&C), uterine surgery, infection, or trauma to the endometrium. These adhesions can affect menstrual flow, cause pelvic pain, and contribute to infertility or recurrent miscarriage.

Treatment Options

Treatment options for removal of adhesions

Treatment is planned to restore the uterine cavity, support healing, and reduce recurrence risk.

Hysteroscopic Adhesiolysis

This minimally invasive procedure uses a hysteroscope and tiny instruments or lasers to cut and remove scar tissues precisely without damaging healthy tissue.

Hormonal Therapy

After surgical removal, estrogen support may be prescribed to stimulate endometrial lining regrowth, support healing, and regulate menstrual cycles.

Post-Surgery Care

A uterine stent or balloon may be placed temporarily, follow-up hysteroscopies may monitor healing, and antibiotics or hormonal therapy may support recovery.
