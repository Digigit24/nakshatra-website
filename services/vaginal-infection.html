Overview

Treating vaginal infections with accurate diagnosis

Vaginal infections can occur due to yeast, bacterial imbalance, hormonal changes, irritants, or hygiene-related triggers. Symptoms may include itching, burning, unusual discharge, odor, pain, or recurrent irritation. At Nakshatra Clinic in Baner, Pune, care focuses on identifying the likely cause, treating symptoms safely, and reducing recurrence with practical prevention guidance.

Symptoms & Causes

Common concerns we evaluate

The original vaginal infection concerns are grouped into clear cards for easier reading on desktop and mobile.

Itching or irritation

Persistent itching, soreness, or redness may point toward yeast infection, irritants, or inflammation.

Unusual discharge

Change in discharge color, consistency, amount, or odor can help identify the infection type.

Burning or discomfort

Burning during urination or discomfort during daily activity may need gynecology review.

Yeast infection

Candida infection is commonly treated with antifungal medication after clinical assessment.

Bacterial imbalance

Bacterial vaginosis may cause odor and discharge and can require antibiotic treatment.

Hormonal changes

Menopause, breastfeeding, or low estrogen states can cause dryness and infection-like irritation.

Treatment Options

Care based on infection type and recurrence

Treatment is selected after reviewing symptoms, examination findings, recurrence pattern, and individual health needs.

Antifungal medication

Topical or oral antifungal medicines may be advised for yeast infections when clinically suitable.

Antibiotic treatment

Antibiotics may be used for bacterial infections such as bacterial vaginosis where indicated.

Hormonal support

Topical estrogen or related care may be discussed for postmenopausal or breastfeeding-related dryness.

Testing when needed

Swab testing or additional evaluation may be advised for recurrent, unclear, or persistent symptoms.

Partner and sexual guidance

Temporary sexual precautions or partner discussion may be recommended based on symptoms and diagnosis.

Prevention follow-up

Breathable clothing, avoiding scented products, hygiene review, and follow-up can reduce recurrence.
